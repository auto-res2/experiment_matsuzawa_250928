**Experimental Design:**
- Strategy: Experiment 1 – Large-scale Benchmark Suite
Objective: Show that the monotone regulariser improves optimisation speed and final regret across a wide spectrum of neural-architecture and tabular MF-HPO benchmarks.

Design
1. Benchmarks
   • LCBench-20 (all 20 tasks, not just 5)  
   • NASBench-201 (3 image datasets, 3 budgets: 25, 50, 100 epochs)  
   • Tabular RL domain (Hyperband-RL, 12 tasks)  
   In total 35 tasks that differ in cost, noise and degree of curve monotonicity.
2. Methods  
   a) Random Search + SHA  
   b) DyHPO (original)  
   c) DyHPO + Mono (ours)  
   d) FastBO, FastBO + Mono (tests plug-in ease) 
3. Budgets: 256 evaluations / task, identical seeds.
4. Metrics  
   • Anytime regret versus wall-clock  
   • Final best regret after 256 evals  
   • RMSE of learning-curve predictions  
   • % of surrogate violations (m(x,b_i)>m(x,b_j) for b_i<b_j)
5. Infrastructure: one A100 per task; tasks run in parallel on 8 GPUs.

Expected Outcome  
• DyHPO+Mono cuts median time-to-target regret by 30–40 % on ≥80 % of tasks.  
• Final regret improves by ≈5 % on average; never worse than DyHPO.  
• Learning-curve RMSE ↓20 % and violation rate ↓85 %.  
• FastBO gains similar improvements, proving drop-in applicability.

Demonstrates: General, statistically significant benefit, negligible overhead, and method portability.

Goal  Demonstrate the generality and portability of the monotone-regularised surrogate on 35 heterogeneous MF-HPO tasks.

      Total → 35 tasks; all are public and come with pre-computed fidelity tables (no simulator needed → reproducible, cheap validation).

B. Methods & Surrogates
   • RS+SHA                (Baseline, no surrogate)
   • DyHPO                 (Deep-kernel GP, variational inference)
   • DyHPO + Mono    (ours; same kernel, λ swept)
   • FastBO                 (Tree-ensemble surrogate)
   • FastBO + Mono    (Tree-ensemble + isotonic regression penalty*).
      *For FastBO we wrap the monotone regulariser around the last linear layer of its embedded GP-head; code provided.

C. Data & Pre-processing
   • Each benchmark already stores validation / test error per configuration & epoch.  We follow official loaders.
   • Normalise objective to y ← (y − y₀)/(y_max − y_min) per task to stabilise GP likelihood.
   • Budgets re-indexed to [1, B] where B is task-specific maximum (#epochs or steps).

D. Splitting & Seeds
   • 256 BO evaluations per task.
   • Ten independent repetitions (seeds = {0,…,9}); identical seeds across all methods → paired tests.
   • Within each repetition, 20 % of observations are held-out (chronologically) for monitoring surrogate RMSE, never shown to BO.

E. Hyper-parameters & Repetitions
   • λ∈{0, 0.03, 0.1, 0.3}.  For each task we pick the λ that minimises validation RMSE averaged over seeds.*  Report both “best-val” and “λ=0.1 fixed”.
   • Surrogate optimiser: Adam (lr = 3e-3, 10 k steps), mini-batch size = 128 pairs.

F. Evaluation Metrics
   Primary   Anytime regret vs wall-clock (↓) – median ± IQR across seeds.
   Secondary  (1) Final regret at 256 evals     (2) Learning-curve RMSE on held-out
              (3) Monotonicity-violation rate   (4) GPU time / memory / FLOPs.

G. Robustness & Ablations
   • Noise-flip test: randomly flip 5 % of logged fidelities – measure Δregret.
   • OOD-budget shift: hide top 10 % budgets at train-time, query acquisition there at test.
   • Hyper-param sweep: sensitivity curves for λ, learning rate, GP length-scale.

EXPERIMENT 3 – REAL-WORLD LARGE-MODEL COST SAVING
============================================================
Goal  Show GPU-hour and dollar savings on an expensive Transformer fine-tuning job.

A. Task & Dataset
   • Model              GPT-2-Medium (355 M params, HF transformers==4.39)
   • Dataset            WikiText-103 (≈ 103 M tokens)
   • Budget             Training epochs b ∈ {1, …, 15}
   • Tokeniser          Byte-level BPE, 50257 vocab.
   • Pre-processing     As in HF example: lowercase, strip accents, chunk to 1024-token blocks.
   • Splits             official train/valid/test (no subsampling).

B. Optimisation Framework
   • Population-Based Training with BO scheduler (PB2)
   • 32 concurrent agents (batch-size 4, gradient-accum 4 → eff. 16) across 8×A100.
   • Surrogate in PB2:
        (a) DyHPO (baseline)
        (b) DyHPO + Mono (λ=0.1)
   • Control knobs = {lr ∈ [3e-5, 1e-4], weight-decay ∈ [0.0, 0.1], dropout ∈ [0.0, 0.2]}

C. Protocol & Seeds
   • 48 h wall-clock cap.
   • Same random seed for data-loader across both runs.
   • Three full repetitions (seeds = 0,1,2) → 6 total PB2 runs.

D. Metrics
   Primary     GPU-hours until validation perplexity ≤ 50.
   Secondary   Final perplexity at 48 h, peak VRAM, surrogate overhead (sec), FLOPs.*
   *FLOPs computed via ptflops per forward+backward and multiplied by #tokens.

E. Early-Stopping & Check-pointing
   • Stop agents that remain >15 perplexity points worse than leader for >4 evals.
   • Best-val checkpoint saved; last checkpoint also archived for robustness analysis.

F. Robustness / Domain-Shift
   • After 48 h, freeze the best model and evaluate on BookCorpus-Open (OOD text) → perplexity reported.

G. Cost Calculation
   • Azure ND-A100 v4 price: 4.10 USD/GPU-hour → expected saving ≈ (40−28)×8×4.10 ≈ $400 per run.

H. Example Scheduler Code (excerpt)
   ```python
   from ray import tune
   from ray.tune.schedulers.pb2 import PB2
   from dyhpo_surrogate import DyHPOSurrogate
   surrogate = DyHPOSurrogate(monotone=True, lam=0.1)
   pb2 = PB2( time_attr='training_iteration', metric='val_ppl', mode='min',
              perturbation_interval=1,  # epoch
              hyperparam_bounds={ 'lr':[3e-5,1e-4], 'weight_decay':[0.0,0.1],
                                   'dropout':[0.0,0.2]},
              surrogate_model=surrogate )
   tune.run(train_gpt2, resources_per_trial={'gpu':1}, num_samples=32,
            scheduler=pb2, stop={'time_total_s': 48*3600})
   ```

To build it, they scraped all the web\npages from outbound links on Reddit which received at least 3 karma. Note that all Wikipedia pages were removed from\nthis dataset, so the model was not trained on any part of Wikipedia. The resulting dataset (called WebText) weights\n40GB of texts but has not been publicly released. You can find a list of the top 1,000 domains present in WebText\n[here](https://github.com/openai/gpt-2/blob/master/domains.txt).\n\n#### Training Procedure\n\nThe model is pretrained on a very large corpus of English data in a self-supervised fashion. This\nmeans it was pretrained on the raw texts only, with no humans labelling them in any way (which is why it can use lots\nof publicly available data) with an automatic process to generate inputs and labels from those texts. More precisely,\nit was trained to guess the next word in sentences.\n\nMore precisely, inputs are sequences of continuous text of a certain length and the targets are the same sequence,\nshifted one token (word or piece of word) to the right. The model uses internally a mask-mechanism to make sure the\npredictions for the token `i` only uses the inputs from `1` to `i` but not the future tokens.\n\nThis way, the model learns an inner representation of the English language that can then be used to extract features\nuseful for downstream tasks.\n\nThe texts are tokenized using a byte-level version of Byte Pair Encoding (BPE) (for unicode characters) and a\nvocabulary size of 50,257. The inputs are sequences of 1024 consecutive tokens.\n\n## Evaluation\n\nThe following evaluation information is extracted from the [associated paper](https://d4mucfpksywv.cloudfront.net/better-language-models/language_models_are_unsupervised_multitask_learners.pdf).\n\n#### Testing Data, Factors and Metrics\n\nThe model authors write in the [associated paper](https://d4mucfpksywv.cloudfront.net/better-language-models/language_models_are_unsupervised_multitask_learners.pdf) that:\n\n> Since our model operates on a byte level and does not require lossy pre-processing or tokenization, we can evaluate it on any language model benchmark.
